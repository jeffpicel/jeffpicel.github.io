Jeffrey Picel

Independent AI researcher · AI safety, reasoning and evaluation

I'm an independent AI researcher. The question I'm working on: can a small network watching a language-model agent mid-task tell that the attempt is heading somewhere bad, early enough to redirect it or stop it? I call these networks critics. A critic reads the model's internal state partway through a task and forecasts how the attempt will end. I use critics to steer reasoning and to guard tool-using agents, for example against instructions planted in the data an agent reads (prompt injection), and I compare them with the usual alternative, training the agent itself by reinforcement learning. A paper is in preparation.

Before this I spent about seven years as a data scientist (DS) and machine learning (ML) engineer, first at Airbnb and then at Faire. At Faire I was the founding data team member (DS and ML/AI) for the brand side of the marketplace (Senior, promoted to Staff in 2023). I was also the data team tech lead for the marketplace quality pillar: five pods over my time there, two or three at once. That work comes down to a trade-off: the cost of missing a harmful brand against the cost of penalizing a good one. By the end we were using generative AI for problems like recognizing the same product across Faire and third-party websites (entity resolution). I also built ranking and causal-measurement systems in production, and co-authored the company's first experimentation guide.

Before industry I did a PhD in economics at Harvard. My dissertation's lead paper used quirks in financial reporting, such as a past return rolling out of the 52-week window the Wall Street Journal printed, to find evidence that investors extrapolate from whichever past returns are most visible to them. Before that I studied mathematics and economics at the University of Chicago.
